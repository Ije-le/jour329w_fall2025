# LESSONS LEARNED

I typically consider LLMs to be useful for journalism, especially for summarizations, extractions and similar tasks. While I would readily list these as common AI tasks though, distilling two years’ worth of stories into a 10-20-page document is not the type of task that would immediately come to my mind. The class strengthened my conviction in AI’s role in journalism and expanded my view of things that are possible in the field using AI tools.

While the scale of the tasks we performed had positive effects on my view of AI, it also influenced some of the biggest challenges I encountered. One significant problem was dealing with rate limits, token and context limits. I lost the progress I had made so many times and started from scratch because of slow models, rate/context limits.  Also, checking for hallucinations was significantly more difficult than usual for me, because of the size of documents involved. Beyond mere hallucinations, getting the LLMs to act right while performing classification tasks was another significant challenge. For instance, models frequently grouped the wrong things together (such as lumping bylines together with sources’ names) due to their capacity as pattern recognition machines.

If I were to give any advice to fellow journalism students, I would reiterate the usual guidelines to keep in mind when using LLMs: every response should be verified, and there should always be a human in the loop. To these, I would add that sometimes, to get the best out of an LLM, especially when working with large documents, more thought should be given to the prompts, as they have significant impact on the number of tokens available to users. They should be crafted in such a way that the information shared is as minimal as possible yet conveys the relevant message to the LLM to avoid token limits and context rot.

More specifically, if performing extraction of information on the kind of scale that we did, I will recommend incrementally saving your files to avoid losing them all in the event progress is lost.
Generally, this course exposed me to the concept of AI agents and their usefulness, embeddings and semantic search. I have also learned that newsrooms can put their archives to good use, not just in the context of generating beat books, but also use llms to generate archival instruments, since the internet itself is not an archive and websites can be attacked/pulled down.
