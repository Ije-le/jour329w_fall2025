## Documenting my choice of 'stories_and_entities_v2_cleaned.json
I updated my stories with entities file to generate new metadata on a random sample of 300 stories: stories_and_entities_v1.json and stories_and_entities_v2.json. v2 is an updated version of v1, so I used it to create my database. When I loaded it to Datasette, I found that bylines were frequently being returned as metadata under names. Chesapeake Publishing, APG Media also got returned as organizations, so I asked copilot to clean these up and ensure that they are not included in the metadata. After copilot did the cleaning, there were fewer cases of authors' names coming back as metadata. There was no APG or Chesapeake Publishing in organizations. It was a much cleaner version than the others I had produced. In creating this file, I had it filtered down to just stories with Eastern Shore as geographical focus. The json file also had the designation for the people named, which I think is good for context, so I chose it for my beat book draft.

# Beatbook Review                              11/21/2025
First thing I noticed and liked about the beatbook is how it describes the sources. It does not just introduce them by their titles, it also includes information on their roles in significant events from the past. This helps a new beat reporter understand the sources better.
I also really like that Public Safety was broken down into topics: EMS and Fire Departments, Law Enforcement and Community Policing, Courts & Justice system, Governments, Policy & Budgeting, Human Trafficking, Infrastructure & Youth. Breaking the beat down in this manner gives a new reporter on this beat a clearer sense of what makes up the beat, but more importantly makes the document a lot easier to follow.
Each topic has a section that highlights recurring issues/patterns, which points reporters in the direction to look for stories. Some expressly use sentences like "... an annual story that can be tied to traffic safety data." Some topics also have an "ongoing issues" section which are a good source for story ideas.
The final sections of the beatbook rounds up these ideas and provides ways that they can be pursued. Under Reporting Workflow, the beatbook highlights ways through which a reporter can source for stories such as scanning the daily 911 log, checking council meeting calendars for specific agenda items like 'burn-ban,' which I think are really good pointers. I think this is a very useful part of the document because not only does it tell you what to do, it includes how to do it.

However, I find that some things are recurrent throughout the document, for example, the burn ban extension, the protest permit debate, these issues came up at least two times in the beatbook. I understand that this is the result of breaking down the beat into topics, so certain issues can overlap, but that can make going it a little redundant.
I also think that a quick summary of the beat at the top could have been a good idea.
One of the beat books I generated for the CNS stories had some information on how sources preferred to be contacted and that could have been useful here as well.
Regardless of these issues which I consider minor, I think this is a very detailed and useful guide.
